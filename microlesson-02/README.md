## The Evaluation Lens: Three Dimensions

> 💡 A promising AI opportunity scores well on **three fronts**:
>
> 1. **Feasibility** – Can it actually be done with GenAI, given the tools, data, and structure you have?
> 2. **Impact** – Will it save time, improve quality, or create real value?
> 3. **Risk** – Are there privacy, ethical, legal, or reputational concerns?

---

### 🛠️ Feasibility: Can we build it?

Ask:
- Do we have the data? Is it structured, accessible, and allowed to be used?
- Is the task language-based or logic-based (a better fit for GenAI)?
- Is there a clear input → output mapping?
- Do off-the-shelf tools (e.g. ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot) already support this?

> ⚠ GenAI is strong with text patterns and summaries—but weak with math, logic, and nuance.

---

### 🚀 Impact: Is it worth doing?

Ask:
- Who benefits? An individual? A team? The org?
- How often is this task done—and how painful is it now?
- Will it save significant time, cost, or cognitive effort?
- Could it unlock something new (e.g. scale insights, synthesize huge inputs)?

> 🧠 Impact isn’t just about speed—it’s about *value* and *scale*.

---

### 🔒 Risk: What could go wrong?

Ask:
- Are we exposing sensitive, private, or regulated data?
- Could the AI mislead someone or make a high-stakes error?
- Might the output contain bias, hallucinations, or outdated knowledge?
- Are we clear about where human oversight is needed?

> ⚠ High-risk = legal, financial, ethical, or reputational stakes without human review.

## Activity: Use Case Evaluation Canvas

Work in small groups. Read each use case scenario and rate it on the 3 categories:
- High / Medium / Low Feasibility
- High / Medium / Low Impact
- High / Medium / Low Risk

Then decide:  
🟢 **Green light** = try it  
🟡 **Yellow** = maybe, with safeguards  
🔴 **Red** = too risky or not feasible
